# Reflection Guide

How to close a feature well (lifecycle stages 8–9). Reflection is short, honest, and
evidence-based. It is where the organization learns — and the only place an improvement to
AOS or the Playbook may originate, as a _proposal_.

## Run Reflection

1. Look back at what actually happened — what was smooth, what was harder than expected, what
   was confusing or repeated.
2. Base it on evidence from this feature, not on general opinion or theoretical ideals.

## Ask the one question

> **"Did we learn something that should improve every future project?"**

- **No** → record a Lesson (`templates/lesson-template.md`) noting what happened, then **close
  the feature.** Most features end here — that is healthy.
- **Yes** → record the Lesson **and** complete the **Future RFC Proposal** section of the same
  template.

## What counts as "Yes"

Only things that would help **every future project** (School Portal, CRM, ERP, POS, HRM,
Booking, Membership, future SaaS) — a recurring gap, a missing standard, an ambiguity that
cost time, a pattern worth making default. One-off, project-specific fixes are a **No**.

## Rules

- **Never edit AOS or the Playbook yourself.** Record a proposal; the Architect decides.
- **Never change anything mid-feature.** Reflection happens after the work, and produces a
  proposal, not an edit.
- **Never propose from speculation or preference.** Every proposal must trace to something that
  really happened in this feature.

After recording the Lesson (and any proposal), the feature is **Done**.
